# What makes a streamer clip get watched

Gathered from the web on 2026-11-23 to write the judge's taste (`studio/judge.py`, `TASTE`). Most of what is
published on this is vendor marketing or one journalist's reading of a chart; items marked *unverified* have
no named dataset behind them. The judge is written from the parts that agree across sources.

## Which moments

1. **Someone embarrassed, failing or caught off guard.** Kotaku's reading of Twitch's most-watched clips:
   "the very fact of someone's embarrassment is enough". Most of the top clips contain no gameplay at all.
   (Journalism from March 2020, not a dataset.)
2. **Danger, shock, a real scare.** Several of the top clips are threats or scares.
3. **A recognisable name.** Fame gives a clip its first push.
4. **Conflict, mocking, trolling.** Listed by the same source.
5. **Genuine surprise, sometimes wholesome.** The single most-watched clip was a surprise reaction.
6. **Clutch plays.** Listed by clip-tool vendors, with no data to rank them. *Unverified.* Gameplay matters
   mostly as context for a reaction.

## Numbers

- **The hook:** vendors agree the first 2 to 3 seconds decide it; a common target is 75-80% of viewers still
  there at 3 seconds. *Unverified*: no official benchmark exists.
- **Length:** most sources land on 20-35 seconds for Shorts; Adobe's March 2026 survey suggests under 30.
  Others argue length matters little next to retention.
- **A view** on Shorts has counted from the moment it starts or replays since 31 March 2025 (documented).
- **What Instagram ranks on:** Adam Mosseri, January 2025: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking
  are watch time, likes and sends", sends mattering most for reaching people who do not follow you.
- **YouTube:** no official weights. Creator-facing statements point to satisfaction, "not interested", likes
  and shares.

## Cues in a transcript

Good: laughter or screaming; a silence and then a shout; "no way", "bro what", "what the", "I'm dead";
talking straight to chat or to a named person; an insult or banter between two people; a payoff that lands
as a reaction.

Boring: explaining with no escalation; reading donations flatly; menus, loading, sponsor reads; a setup that
runs past its payoff.

## Hooks

Short (the phone feed cuts titles at about 40 characters), a curiosity gap the clip really closes, the
person or the stakes named. Whether capitals help is *unverified*.
